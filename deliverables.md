# Sembla Deliverables

Date: 2026-03-06

## Purpose

This file defines what Sembla actually delivers for each offer and how the work is executed.

Use it as the operational reference when:

- shaping site copy
- writing decks
- scoping inquiries
- pricing work
- rebuilding product or workflow systems later

## Core Delivery Principle

Sembla does not deliver "just images."
Sembla delivers commercially usable likeness with rights, approvals, provenance, and control attached.

Every offer should resolve into:

- a defined commercial scope
- a clear approval state
- a traceable delivery package
- a reusable rights record

## 1. Likeness Licensing

### What the client is buying

Commercial access to represented talent for a defined campaign or usage window.

### Exact deliverables

- talent shortlist or approved talent selection
- booking memo
- rights scope schedule
- consent and approval pack
- creative alignment notes
- final agreed outputs or licensed campaign use
- license reference ID
- governance handoff summary

### What each deliverable means

- Talent shortlist or approved talent selection:
  the represented options that fit the brief, category, and usage
- Booking memo:
  the commercial summary of who is booked, for what, under which scope
- Rights scope schedule:
  territory, term, media, exclusivity, channels, and renewal logic
- Consent and approval pack:
  documented approval state, restrictions, and use boundaries
- Creative alignment notes:
  references, styling direction, prompt direction, and brand guardrails
- Final agreed outputs or licensed campaign use:
  the stills, selective video, or usage approval covered by the booking
- License reference ID:
  the internal and client-facing identifier tied to the talent profile and deal
- Governance handoff summary:
  a concise record of what was approved, delivered, and still restricted

### How we do it

1. Receive the brief.
2. Match the right represented talent to the use case.
3. Define the commercial scope before production moves.
4. Capture talent approvals and restrictions.
5. Align the creative and delivery plan.
6. Deliver the outputs together with the approval and usage package.

## 2. Digital Twin Build

### What the client is buying

A consent-led digital likeness build for repeatable commercial use.

### Exact deliverables

- approved source material set
- twin build specification
- aesthetic and manipulation guardrails
- test outputs and approval rounds
- approved digital twin
- initial production-ready outputs
- twin usage notes

### What each deliverable means

- Approved source material set:
  the image, video, and reference inputs cleared for use in the build
- Twin build specification:
  the internal setup notes for how the likeness is structured and intended to perform
- Aesthetic and manipulation guardrails:
  explicit limits on styling, body edits, face edits, category use, and visual boundaries
- Test outputs and approval rounds:
  the validation stage before the twin is approved for commercial use
- Approved digital twin:
  the likeness system ready for controlled production use
- Initial production-ready outputs:
  the first still or selective video assets generated from the approved twin
- Twin usage notes:
  what the twin can and cannot be used for within the agreed scope

### How we do it

1. Define the intended commercial use.
2. Confirm that the source material and approvals cover the build.
3. Build the twin against the agreed aesthetic and category guardrails.
4. Run test outputs and gather approval feedback.
5. Finalize the twin only after approval.
6. Deliver the approved twin outputs together with usage notes.

## 3. Stewardship & Governance

### What the client is buying

Ongoing management of reuse, localization, renewals, provenance, disclosure, and approvals after the first campaign or twin build.

### Exact deliverables

- updated and localized assets
- versioning plan across markets and channels
- renewed approval trail
- usage renewal record
- provenance and disclosure support
- governance status summary

### What each deliverable means

- Updated and localized assets:
  approved new versions for market, language, format, or seasonal use
- Versioning plan across markets and channels:
  which asset belongs to which market, platform, or commercial scope
- Renewed approval trail:
  the record of any additional talent approvals or restriction changes
- Usage renewal record:
  what has been extended, renewed, or newly authorized
- Provenance and disclosure support:
  the supporting record for source, generation context, and disclosure readiness
- Governance status summary:
  the current state of approvals, renewals, restrictions, and active usage

### How we do it

1. Review what is live and what needs to expand.
2. Identify new markets, channels, or asset variants.
3. Update approvals and usage scope before output delivery.
4. Generate, review, and version new assets.
5. Hand off the updated governance record together with the new assets.

## 4. Digital Rights Representation

Status:

- strategic offer
- supply-side focused
- not necessarily a public headline offer yet

### What the client is buying

Representation and management of a talent's digital rights, restrictions, and approval logic.

### Exact deliverables

- digital rights profile
- restriction framework
- category and competitor exclusions
- approval logic map
- monetization and reuse guidelines
- rights negotiation support

### What each deliverable means

- Digital rights profile:
  a structured record of what the likeness can be used for
- Restriction framework:
  explicit limits on aesthetic use, manipulation, brand use, and category fit
- Category and competitor exclusions:
  the commercial exclusions attached to the likeness
- Approval logic map:
  who must approve what, and at which stage
- Monetization and reuse guidelines:
  the commercial principles for repeat use and extension
- Rights negotiation support:
  support in shaping digital-use deal terms

### How we do it

1. Map the talent's comfort zone, boundaries, and goals.
2. Convert those into structured rules instead of vague preferences.
3. Use those rules in brand negotiations and campaign scoping.
4. Update the rights profile as the talent relationship evolves.

## 5. Protection & Takedowns

Status:

- secondary strategic offer
- likely partner-led at first

### What the client is buying

Monitoring, misuse response, and takedown support around digital likeness abuse.

### Exact deliverables

- misuse review log
- incident record
- takedown package
- audit trail support
- escalation recommendation

### What each deliverable means

- Misuse review log:
  a record of suspected unauthorized uses
- Incident record:
  the stored details of what happened, where, and how it was verified
- Takedown package:
  the evidence and request material needed for enforcement action
- Audit trail support:
  the supporting record of ownership, approval state, and authorized use
- Escalation recommendation:
  whether to monitor, request removal, or pursue stronger action

### How we do it

1. Detect or receive notice of suspected misuse.
2. Verify whether the use is authorized.
3. Assemble the supporting rights and provenance record.
4. Prepare and send the takedown or escalation package.
5. Track outcome and retain the audit trail.

## Standard Components That Should Travel Across Offers

Where applicable, every Sembla engagement should try to include:

- rights scope
- approval record
- license reference ID
- provenance-ready documentation
- deliverable inventory
- restrictions summary

These components are part of the moat.
They should become structured data later, not just PDFs and email threads.

## What Is Not Included By Default

Unless specifically scoped, Sembla should not imply that every engagement includes:

- unlimited revisions
- open-ended usage
- perpetual rights
- unrestricted global rollout
- takedown and monitoring
- custom software or client portal access

Those should always be explicitly scoped.
