# Market Research & TAM

Date: 2026-03-06

## Core Conclusion

The current public sector set should be treated as Sembla's beachhead, not the ceiling of the business.

The right early wedge is:

- Fashion
- Beauty
- Hospitality
- Spirits

These sectors do not make Sembla look narrow as long as they are framed as `where we work best`, not as the only markets the company can serve.

## Why This Is The Right Beachhead

- These sectors buy premium image systems repeatedly across launches, markets, and formats.
- They care about talent access, rights clarity, controlled reuse, and brand safety.
- They can justify premium spend when travel, reshoots, approvals, and territory issues are reduced.
- They align with Sembla's current edge: represented talent, clear rights, and repeatable digital output.

## Beachhead vs TAM

### Beachhead Markets

- luxury fashion and capsule collaborations
- beauty and ambassador-led brand campaigns
- luxury hospitality and travel brands
- premium spirits and tightly scoped ambassador activations

### Expansion Markets

- jewelry and watches
- branded residences
- private aviation
- premium automotive
- private membership and concierge brands
- cultural partnerships and branded activations
- talent-side digital rights representation
- estate and archive licensing
- white-label likeness infrastructure for agencies and talent firms

The beachhead is large enough to build a serious company.
The long-term TAM is much broader than the current four sector tiles.

## Music-Adjacent Recommendation

Music-adjacent is strategically strong, but it should not be a top-line `Best fit` tile yet unless Sembla is actively selling into labels, managers, artist teams, or music partnership budgets right now.

For now, music-adjacent should be treated as:

- an adjacent market in company strategy
- a strong supply-side advantage because multi-hyphenate music talent strengthens the roster
- a cultural partnership lane for brand campaigns, festivals, ambassador work, and cross-category activations

Why this is the right balance:

- it keeps the demand-side story focused on premium brand buyers
- it preserves room for music, culture, and entertainment opportunities
- it avoids making the public site feel split between luxury brand services and entertainment services

## Public-Site Recommendation

Keep the current four sectors on the site, but do not frame them as the full market.

Recommended wording change:

- replace `Best fit` with `Where we work best` or `Strongest early fit`
- add a quiet support line such as `Also relevant for adjacent premium sectors and cultural partnerships on request.`

If music should become more visible later, add it as a case study or a cultural partnership scenario before adding it as a fifth top-line sector.

## North Star

Sembla should not think of its market as `AI modeling`.

The real market is:

`commercial likeness infrastructure for premium brands and high-value talent`

The site can stay selective.
The company can still pursue a much larger TAM underneath that surface.
