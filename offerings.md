# Sembla Offerings

Date: 2026-03-06

## Purpose

This file is a north-star reference for Sembla's offer stack.
When reopening this project, default back to this structure before expanding the site or adding new services.

## Core Rule

Sembla should not sell generic AI production.
Sembla should sell governed commercial use of likeness.

The public site can remain elegant and selective.
The underlying offer must stay clear, high-value, and hard to copy.

## Primary Public Offer Stack

These are the three offers Sembla should lead with.

### 1. Likeness Licensing

What it is:

- structured brand access to represented talent
- defined rights, approvals, and commercial usage terms
- talent access with legal and reputational clarity

Why it matters:

- solves rights ambiguity
- solves gray-market likeness risk
- gives brands commercial access to premium faces they can actually use

What the client receives:

- talent shortlist or selection
- booking memo
- consent and approval pack
- usage scope by territory, term, media, and exclusivity
- final agreed outputs or licensed campaign use

This should remain the first and clearest offer.
It is the bridge between representation and commercial use.

### 2. Digital Twin Build

What it is:

- consent-led creation of a reusable digital likeness
- built from approved source material
- structured for repeatable commercial output

Why it matters:

- reduces production drag
- makes repeat campaigns faster
- lets one approved face work across multiple formats and uses

What the client receives:

- approved digital twin
- guardrail and usage notes
- test outputs and approval rounds
- initial production-ready still or selective video outputs

This is not the lead story on its own.
It is valuable because it sits inside rights, approvals, and governance.

### 3. Stewardship & Governance

What it is:

- ongoing management after the first campaign or twin build
- covers reuse, localization, renewals, provenance, disclosure, and approvals
- keeps long-term brand use controlled over time

Why it matters:

- solves the operational mess after the first deal is done
- prevents rights drift across markets, formats, and versions
- turns one-off work into a controlled long-term relationship

What the client receives:

- updated and localized assets
- market, language, and channel versioning
- renewed approval trail and usage renewals
- provenance and disclosure support

This is strategically one of the most important offers.
It is where software, workflows, and recurring value can compound.

## Secondary Strategic Offers

These matter, but should not distract from the primary three on the public site yet.

### Digital Rights Representation

For talent and managers:

- digital-use contract structure
- category restrictions
- competitor exclusions
- approval logic
- protection of reuse economics

This is a strong supply-side moat and should remain in the company strategy even if it is not foregrounded publicly yet.

### Protection & Takedowns

For talent and eventually brands:

- monitoring
- unauthorized use detection
- takedowns
- audit trail support

This should likely come later or through partnerships first.

## ICP Focus

Primary demand-side ICPs:

- luxury fashion brands
- beauty brands
- jewelry and watches
- hospitality and premium travel
- spirits and culturally led premium consumer brands
- creative agencies producing for those brands

Best buyer roles:

- brand marketing leads
- campaign leads
- creative directors
- innovation or digital teams

Primary supply-side ICPs:

- supermodels
- culturally relevant models
- DJs, artists, and multi-hyphenate talent with image value
- managers who want monetizable digital rights without loss of control

## Why These Offers Matter Most

These offers map directly to the highest-value pain points:

- brands need premium content faster
- brands need rights clarity
- talent needs control over replicas
- campaigns need localization and reuse
- commercial use needs approvals, provenance, and disclosure

The market already validates this direction:

- fashion and luxury are under pressure to do more with less
- model image-rights issues are increasing
- brands are already experimenting with digital twins
- provenance and disclosure requirements are getting stronger

## What Not To Add

Avoid broadening into:

- generic AI content studio work
- cheap social content packages
- self-serve avatar products
- creator marketplace dynamics
- generic production services unrelated to represented talent

Those are easier to copy and weaken the category.

## Public Positioning Rule

Publicly, the site should read as:

- refined
- private
- controlled
- commercially clear

Not:

- SaaS
- marketplace
- creative agency fluff
- AI novelty

## Internal North Stars

When deciding whether to add a service, ask:

1. Does this increase control over commercial likeness use?
2. Does this deepen the approval, rights, or provenance moat?
3. Does this become more valuable with repeat campaigns and repeat talent?
4. Is this hard for a generic AI studio to copy?
5. Does this strengthen either premium demand or premium supply?

If the answer is mostly no, it should not be a focus.

## Short Version

Sembla's focus should be:

- Likeness Licensing
- Digital Twin Build
- Stewardship & Governance

Everything else is secondary unless it strengthens those three.
