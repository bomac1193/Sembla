# Sembla Simulated Beta Test

Date: 2026-03-06

## Important Note

This is a simulated beta test, not real user research.

It is based on:

- the current state of the Sembla site and offer stack
- current market signals in luxury, fashion, beauty, hospitality, spirits, and adjacent premium sectors
- the likely reactions of buying roles, talent-side stakeholders, skeptics, and critics

Use this as directional strategy input, not as a substitute for real interviews.

## Scope

- 30 simulated current ICP reviewers
- 10 simulated adjacent-market reviewers from high-fit sectors not yet fully targeted
- critics and anti-AI skeptics included across both groups
- each reviewer scored the current site and service stack out of 10

## Score Summary

- Core ICP average: `7.3 / 10`
- Adjacent missed-market average: `7.4 / 10`
- Critics / haters average: `5.1 / 10`
- Highest-resonance offer: `Likeness Licensing`
- Second-highest resonance: `Digital Twin Build`
- Most strategically valued but least instantly understood: `Stewardship & Governance`

## What People Consistently Loved

- The brand feels premium, private, and differentiated from generic AI studios.
- `Selected Talent` is a stronger proof layer than a pure strategy site.
- `Likeness Licensing` lands quickly with brand, legal, and talent-side buyers.
- The inquiry path is simple and appropriately discreet.
- The rights-first angle gives the company a real business reason to exist.

## What People Consistently Hated

- Too little proof for such a high-trust offer.
- Some language still feels conceptual instead of commercial.
- `Stewardship & Governance` sounds important but needs examples.
- The public `Admin` link feels internal and weakens luxury positioning.
- There is no visible case study, process map, or sample deliverable.

## Strongest Missed Markets

These adjacent sectors showed the highest fit:

- luxury hospitality
- branded residences
- premium spirits
- watches and jewelry
- private aviation and membership brands

Why:

- they already rely on premium ambassadors and image-heavy campaigns
- they benefit from repeatable regional content
- they care about brand control and reputational safety
- they operate with higher margins and can buy managed services

## Lowest-Scoring Objections

The harshest critics converged on the same problems:

- too little proof for a trust-heavy offer
- too much abstract language in some places
- not enough visible process
- no case study, sample pack, or proof artifact
- public-facing internal cues like `Admin`

## What To Implement Now

1. Remove the public `Admin` link from buyer-facing navigation.
2. Add one `How it works` strip:
   - Brief
   - Scope and approval
   - Deliver and govern
3. Add one sample proof artifact:
   - redacted usage scope
   - redacted consent pack
   - redacted deliverable inventory
4. Add one use-case row by sector:
   - fashion
   - beauty
   - hospitality
   - spirits
5. Add one light case-study format:
   - challenge
   - structure
   - outputs
   - governance
6. Make `Stewardship & Governance` easier to grasp with one concrete example.
7. Add service-specific inquiry presets for each offer and sector.

## What To Reduce

- abstract category language
- repeated explanation of the same rights idea
- any product-dashboard cues on the public site
- overuse of the word `private`
- overemphasis on AI mechanics instead of business outcomes

## What Not To Add Yet

- public pricing
- self-serve product demo
- extra services beyond the three core offers
- generic AI content studio language
- broad creator marketplace framing

## ERRC Matrix

### Eliminate

- public `Admin` navigation
- leftover internal or operational UI language
- repeated rights-theory copy that does not add trust
- any marketplace or inventory-style cues

### Reduce

- abstract framing
- long explanatory intros
- jargon that requires interpretation
- the gap between promise and proof

### Raise

- commercial specificity
- visible trust signals
- deliverable clarity
- sector-specific relevance
- proof of approvals, rights, and governance

### Create

- case studies
- sample proof artifacts
- a process strip
- sector-specific use cases
- a clearer proof layer for stewardship and governance

## Working Conclusion

The positioning is directionally strong.
The site already feels more premium and more differentiated than a generic AI studio.

What keeps it below a 9 or 10 is not the brand.
It is the proof gap.

Sembla is strongest when it says:

- represented talent
- clear rights
- repeatable commercial use
- approvals and governance attached

Sembla gets weaker when it sounds like:

- an abstract category thesis
- a soft strategy consultancy
- an AI tool with luxury styling
