# Sembla Strategy

Date: 2026-03-06

## Core Thesis

Sembla should look like a private roster on the surface and operate like commercial likeness infrastructure underneath.

The site can stay elegant, editorial, and selective.
The company should be built around a harder promise:

- premium talent access
- explicit consent for digital replicas
- campaign-safe usage rights
- provenance and disclosure
- ongoing twin management and protection

This is stronger than "AI model agency" and harder to copy than "avatar platform."

## Why Now

- Fashion growth remains weak. McKinsey's State of Fashion 2025 says revenue growth is expected to stay in the low single digits and only 20 percent of executives expected better consumer sentiment in 2025.
- Luxury has slowed. McKinsey's State of Luxury 2025 says sector growth is expected at 1 to 3 percent annually through 2027, with more than 80 percent of recent growth driven by price increases rather than volume.
- The modeling industry is actively reorganizing around image rights. The New York Fashion Workers Act took effect on June 19, 2025 and includes consent requirements around digital replicas.
- Model Alliance says 87 percent of surveyed fashion workers reported concern about AI's negative impacts, including image abuse, uncompensated reuse, and job displacement.
- Major brands are already normalizing digital twins. H&M publicly released AI imagery built with model digital twins in July 2025.
- Provenance and disclosure are becoming table stakes. The EU AI Act requires certain synthetic outputs to be machine-readable and deepfake uses to be disclosed, while C2PA / Content Credentials are emerging as the practical provenance layer.

## The Real Problem

Fashion and luxury do not mainly have an image-generation problem.
They have a trust, control, and rights problem.

Brands want:

- faster campaigns
- lower production drag
- premium faces
- localized assets
- less legal ambiguity

Talent wants:

- control over digital replicas
- explicit approval rights
- better economics on reuse
- less exploitation by agencies, brands, and gray-market AI actors
- protection from unauthorized copies and deepfakes

Sembla should sit in the middle and make that exchange safe, legible, and scalable.

## Biggest Problems Ahead

### 1 Year

- Margin pressure will push brands to replace expensive shoots with AI-assisted production.
- More brands will experiment with model twins and synthetic campaigns.
- Models and managers will become more alert to unauthorized training, image scraping, and replica misuse.
- Agencies will look slow if they only sell bookings and do not sell digital rights.

### 5 Years

- Digital twins for top talent will be normal in campaign production.
- Rights, disclosure, and provenance will matter more than novelty.
- The winning companies will own approval workflows, usage rules, and audit trails.
- Premium brands will want one partner that can handle talent access, synthetic production, localization, and compliance.

### 10 Years

- Image generation will be cheap and abundant.
- Scarcity will shift from production capability to controlled identity, trusted provenance, and legally durable rights.
- Supermodels, artists, and culturally important talent will treat their digital likeness like licensable infrastructure.
- The valuable company will not be the one that can make the twin. It will be the one that controls the permissioned graph around the twin.

## Modeling And Supermodel Industry Pain Points

- Models have historically lacked labor protections and visibility into contracts, fees, and client relationships.
- Digital replica usage can create uncompensated reuse at scale if not contractually governed.
- Top talent is constrained by geography, schedule, travel, overexposure risk, and exclusivity conflicts.
- Managers and agencies are not structurally set up for versioned digital rights, AI approvals, or always-on content licensing.
- Luxury brands want famous or culturally resonant faces, but they do not want legal ambiguity or reputational blowback.

This is where Sembla has room to become category-defining:
not a casting database, but a trusted digital extension of elite representation.

## Current ICPs

These are the customers Sembla currently reads best for:

- luxury fashion brands
- beauty brands
- music-adjacent campaigns and brand collaborations
- creative agencies producing for premium brands
- talent managers or model managers curious about AI-safe monetization

## Best ICPs Now

Demand side:

- brand marketing, campaign, and creative leads at premium brands
- innovation or digital teams inside luxury, beauty, jewelry, hospitality, and spirits
- creative agencies that need premium visuals faster without gray-market likeness risk

Supply side:

- culturally relevant models, supermodels, DJs, artists, and multi-hyphenates
- talent with strong image value and repeat brand demand
- managers who want to monetize digital use without surrendering control

## Best ICPs Later

- enterprise luxury groups running multi-market content programs
- talent agencies that need white-label likeness operations
- estates, archives, or legacy talent rights holders
- premium ecommerce and retail groups needing localized always-on campaign assets

## Services To Offer

### Offer Now

- private roster access
- campaign licensing
- AI-assisted still and selective video campaign production
- usage scoping by territory, term, media, and exclusivity
- consent and approval packs
- provenance and disclosure handoff

### Offer Next

- digital twin build
- twin refresh and stewardship retainers
- localization and versioning across markets
- partner-supported monitoring and takedowns
- structured brand guardrails per talent

### Offer Later

- Sembla Likeness Passport
- client portal for approvals and usage history

## Blue Ocean Direction

Do not compete as:

- an AI photo studio
- a generic avatar SaaS
- a talent marketplace
- a normal modeling agency with AI as a side feature

Compete as:

`The private likeness layer for premium brands and high-value talent.`

That category is still open.

## What Makes Sembla Uncopyable

### 1. Exclusive Inputs

If you have the right talent and the right contracts, your raw material is proprietary.
Anyone can generate images.
Not everyone can legally generate those specific faces.

### 2. Permission Graph

The moat is not "a digital twin."
The moat is:

- who approved what
- for which use
- in which market
- for how long
- with what restrictions
- with what provenance trail

That data compounds and becomes hard to recreate.

### 3. White-Glove Operations Before Software

Run the messy approvals, brand fit, usage scoping, and exception handling manually first.
Software comes after the workflow is real.
This gives Sembla a service moat before a product moat.

### 4. Premium Network Effects

As better talent joins, better brands come in.
As better brands come in, stronger talent economics become possible.
That loop is much stronger than a commodity marketplace loop.

### 5. Trust Infrastructure

If Sembla becomes known for:

- consent-first deal structure
- provenance attached to every asset
- disclosure-ready outputs
- takedown and enforcement support

then it becomes the safe default for premium AI likeness work.

## Revolutionary Moves

These are the ideas with the most strategic upside.

### Sembla Likeness Passport

A persistent rights and provenance layer attached to each talent identity:

- approved use cases
- restricted categories
- territory and term logic
- consent history
- C2PA / Content Credentials-ready provenance
- brand-safe disclosure notes

This is much more defensible than a static roster card.

### Twin Stewardship, Not Twin Delivery

Do not stop at "we built the twin."
Own:

- change requests
- retraining
- market localization
- usage renewal
- deactivation
- post-campaign monitoring

That turns a one-off sale into operating leverage.

### Premium Localization Engine

Use one approved twin to generate region-specific luxury campaign assets across:

- markets
- languages
- formats
- channel mixes

This solves a real brand budget and speed problem.

### Talent-Controlled Guardrails

Give talent and managers explicit control over:

- aesthetic boundaries
- category exclusions
- competitor restrictions
- body and face manipulation limits
- approval thresholds

This becomes part of the product, not just the contract.

### Protection Layer

Eventually add or partner for:

- monitoring
- unauthorized use detection
- takedowns
- audit trails

Protection closes the loop. It turns Sembla from monetization partner into identity steward.

## Positioning Recommendation

Publicly:

- keep the site refined, private, and roster-led
- speak to brands in the language of access, rights clarity, and commercial use
- avoid sounding like a SaaS dashboard

Operationally:

- build toward a likeness rights and twin management business
- keep detailed workflow, approval, and provenance data from day one
- treat every booking like the seed of a reusable rights record

## What Not To Do

- do not become a broad creator marketplace
- do not compete on cheap content volume
- do not oversell "AI innovation" as the value proposition
- do not present models as generic inventory
- do not build software before the approval and rights workflow is deeply understood

## Short Version

Sembla should start as a private agency for rights-cleared digital talent.
It should evolve into the trusted operating layer for premium digital likeness.

The winning insight is:

The future value is not in rendering faces.
It is in owning the safest, most elegant system for who gets to use them, how, where, and under what proof.
